Property Investment Model: Scenario Analysis & IRR Sensitivity

This memorandum presents the underlying logic, assumptions, and scenario sensitivities for the proposed property investment model. The intent is to transition our acquisition dialogue away from abstract machine-learning accuracy claims (e.g., $R^2$ scores) toward a disciplined, hard-nosed evaluation of absolute cashflow and downside risk. Properties are not valuable because a regression model says they are; they are valuable because of the cash they generate and the capital they protect. Our data models must be subordinated to this reality.

1. The Opportunity and The Underwriting Baseline

The proposed quantitative model ingests standard housing features (square footage, locality indices, structural age) to output an expected market acquisition price. While the algorithmic prediction component is sound (verifying against a historical baseline with an $R^2$ of 0.87), treating the predicted price as the sole decision criteria invites disaster. A high-accuracy model merely tells us what the market *is* paying. It does not tell us whether the market is *right*, nor does it confirm if the yield justifies the capital outlay.

We are underwriting this acquisition model against the following baseline assumptions over a standard 5-year hold period:

  • Capital Outlay: Expected model prediction = $100 \%$ acquisition cost.
  • LTV (Loan-to-Value): $65\%$, amortized over 25 years at a fixed debt cost of $6.5\%$.
  • Terminal Capitalization Rate: $5.50\%$, reflecting a conservative exit penalty.
  • Vacancy & Credit Loss: $5.0\%$ of Gross Potential Rent (GPR) annually.
  • Operating Expenses (OpEx): Modeled at $35\%$ of Effective Gross Income (EGI).

2. The Cashflow Ladder Logic

To justify a purchase decision, the predicted price from the model must feed directly into the cashflow ladder. The cashflow ladder is the mechanism that proves whether the price demanded by the seller transforms into an acceptable yield or destroys equity. The Cashflow Ladder. The predictive model only prices the asset; the ladder prices the operation. If Net Operating Income (NOI) cannot safely clear the debt service coverage threshold following conservative vacancy and OpEx assumptions, the model's acquisition price recommendation must be overridden and rejected.

3. Return Metrics from First Principles: Internal Rate of Return (IRR)

The IC requires a minimum Levered IRR of $15.0\%$ to approve an acquisition. The IRR is not a static number-it is the discount rate that drops the Net Present Value (NPV) of all future cash flows down to exactly zero. It acts as our hurdle rate. If the IRR exceeds our cost of capital, wealth is created. If it falls short, we are destroying equity.

Our model programmatically extracts the predicted acquisition cost and runs it against a simulated 5-year operating ledger. The terminal value relies entirely on the assumed exit Cap Rate, making the calculation intensely sensitive to timing and market exit conditions.

# IRR Calculation Engine: Tying the predictive model to the cashflow model
import numpy as np

def calculate_irr(predicted_price, down_payment_pct, yr1_noi, growth_rate, exit_cap):
    # Initial equity outflow
    equity_outlay = -(predicted_price * down_payment_pct)
    
    # Operational cash flows (Years 1 to 5)
    cash_flows = [equity_outlay]
    noi = yr1_noi
    for year in range(5):
        # Simplified: Assuming interest-only debt service for clarity here
        debt_service = (predicted_price * (1 - down_payment_pct)) * 0.065
        levered_cf = noi - debt_service
        cash_flows.append(levered_cf)
        noi *= (1 + growth_rate)
        
    # Terminal Value in Year 5 (NOI / Exit Cap Rate) - Debt Payoff
    terminal_value = (noi / exit_cap) - (predicted_price * (1 - down_payment_pct))
    cash_flows[-1] += terminal_value
    
    # Yield the IRR
    return np.irr(cash_flows) * 100

4. Scenario Analysis and Sensitivity Pressure

A deterministic prediction is worthless in an environment defined by volatility. The primary risk to this business model is not that the algorithm incorrectly predicts today's price by $2\%$. The risk is that macroeconomic headwinds compress rents, spike vacancies, or expand exit Cap Rates, wiping out downside equity.

We stress-test the model's target price through a two-dimensional sensitivity grid. Because real estate valuations are highly levered, a $5\%$ miss on rental growth compounded over five years can compress the IRR by several hundred basis points.

These mechanics strip away the safety implied by "accurate" ML regressions. If the capital stack is misaligned with operating reality, a highly accurate acquisition price simply guarantees an accurate loss.

5. Recommendation and Discipline

The quantitative model is an input, not an underwriter. We recommend proceeding with acquisitions scored by this model only if the asset clears the $15.0\%$ IRR hurdle after the predicted price is subjected to the following downside stress tests:

  1. OpEx assumptions are inflated by an additional $15\%$ over historical trendlines to account for inflationary pressures on labor, materials, and insurance.
  2. Vacancy rates are stressed to $8.0\%$, simulating cyclical softening.
  3. Exit Cap Rate is expanded by $50$ basis points over entry, eliminating the assumption that we can rely on multiple-expansion to engineer returns.

If the model’s target acquisition price cannot survive these operational impacts while preserving equity, the asset must be discarded. We are in the business of pricing risk, and any valuation model that treats risk as a static coefficient in a pandas dataframe fundamentally misunderstands the mandate of this firm.
